10 Strategies to Simplify Fitness

Grocery shopping, meal prepping, and sticking to a workout schedule can feel like a second full-time job. Dr. Joey shares 10 practical strategies that make staying consistent feel effortless instead of overwhelming.

Dr. Joey Munoz, PhD
· August 26, 2026 · 9 min read
Quick Answer

Ten strategies can make fitness feel effortless instead of overwhelming: on nutrition, buy pre-marinated protein, batch cook instead of individually plating meals, keep a couple of go-to meal templates, use grocery delivery, and stock healthy snacks. On training, do full-body sessions 2-3x a week, stick to compound movements, use supersets, warm up with your first exercise instead of cardio and stretching, and keep your program simple.

You know you need to prioritize your nutrition and workouts, but grocery shopping, meal prepping every week, and sticking to a regular training program can feel like a second full-time job, especially with a career and a family. Here are 10 strategies to make it feel effortless instead.

5 Nutrition Strategies

1. Buy Pre-Marinated or Pre-Cooked Protein

Making all your protein from scratch is daunting and unnecessary. Most grocery stores sell pre-marinated meats for a small premium, and stores like Costco carry fully pre-cooked options like rotisserie chicken or sirloin steak. It costs a little more, but it saves real time and adds variety without extra effort.

2. Meal Prep Through Batch Cooking

Instead of plating individual meals into a dozen containers, pick one or two protein sources, one or two carbs, and one or two veggies, and cook each in bulk. Store them separately, then mix and match throughout the week. This is faster, creates less waste, and gives you more meal variety than pre-portioned plates.

3. Have a Few Go-To Meal Templates

Constantly deciding what to eat is mentally exhausting. Build two or three repeatable meals using a simple structure: roughly a third protein, a third veggies, a third carbs, aiming for 40 to 50 grams of protein per meal. Rotating through a few reliable meals removes the daily decision fatigue entirely.

4. Invest in Grocery or Meal Delivery

Grocery shopping can easily eat two to three hours of your week. If your schedule and budget allow it, delivery services free up that time for work, family, or meal prep itself, which is often a better trade than the cost of delivery.

5. Keep Healthy Snacks Readily Available

You're far more likely to reach for whatever's easiest to access. Stocking your fridge and pantry with Greek yogurt, jerky, fresh fruit, and pre-boiled eggs means the convenient option is also the one that fits your goals.

5 Training Strategies

1. Train Full Body 2-3x a Week Instead of a Complicated Split

Cutting your weekly gym visits down while doing longer, higher-volume full-body sessions each time reduces commuting time and scheduling stress dramatically, without sacrificing results, since the total weekly volume can stay the same.

2. Stick to Compound Movements

Movements like squats, deadlifts, presses, and rows work multiple muscles at once, meaning fewer total exercises are needed to cover your whole body effectively. Unless you're training specifically for bodybuilding, extensive isolation work isn't necessary.

3. Use Supersets to Cut Workout Time

Pair two exercises that train opposite muscle groups, like a bench press and pull-ups, and alternate between them with shorter rest periods. This can cut a workout that would normally take 30 minutes down to about 20, without sacrificing quality.

4. Warm Up With Your First Exercise, Not Cardio or Stretching

Static stretching before lifting has little proven benefit, and dedicated cardio warm-ups aren't necessary either. Warming up progressively with lighter sets of your actual first exercise prepares your body just as effectively, without adding extra time.

5. Keep Your Program Simple and Repeatable

A program you can actually remember and execute consistently beats a complicated one you abandon within a month. Simplicity supports adherence, and adherence is what actually drives results.

Instead of trying to figure something new out every single day, having a simple, repeatable system removes the friction that usually derails consistency.
Key Takeaways
  • Pre-marinated protein and batch cooking dramatically cut meal prep time without sacrificing variety.
  • A couple of reliable go-to meals removes daily decision fatigue around nutrition.
  • Full-body training 2-3x a week with compound movements can match the results of a more frequent split.
  • Supersets and skipping unnecessary stretching or cardio warm-ups shorten workouts without cutting effectiveness.
  • Simplicity and repeatability, not complexity, are what actually support long-term consistency.

None of these strategies require more discipline, they require less friction. Building systems that make the healthy choice the easy choice is what actually makes fitness sustainable for a genuinely busy life.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is batch cooking and how does it simplify meal prep?

Batch cooking means preparing one or two protein sources, carb sources, and vegetable sources in large quantities, then mixing and matching them into meals throughout the week, rather than assembling and plating individual portioned meals in advance.

Is it better to train 5 days a week or 3 days a week if you're busy?

Training full body 2 to 3 times a week with longer, higher-volume sessions can produce equivalent results to training 5 days a week, while cutting down significantly on commuting time and making the schedule far easier to maintain.

How do supersets save time in the gym?

Pairing two exercises that work opposite muscle groups and alternating between them with shorter rest periods lets you complete both exercises in less total time than doing them one after another with full rest periods.

Do you need to stretch or do cardio before lifting weights?

Not necessarily. Warming up with lighter sets of your first exercise is an effective way to prepare your body for training, and static stretching before lifting has little proven benefit for performance or injury prevention.
