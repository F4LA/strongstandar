The Minimum Effective Workout Plan

You want to lose fat and build muscle, but you don't have hours to spend in the gym. Dr. Joey breaks down a science-backed plan built specifically for people with real schedules.

Dr. Joey Munoz, PhD
· August 21, 2026 · 8 min read
Quick Answer

You don't need hours in the gym to lose fat effectively. Three full-body strength sessions a week under 60 minutes, two short high-intensity interval sessions of 10 to 20 minutes, and a daily step goal cover the core bases exercise needs to hit for fat loss.

If you're trying to lose fat and build muscle but don't have time for two-hour gym sessions four or five days a week, here's the good news: with an effective plan, you can get excellent results in far less time than most people assume is required.

First, an important distinction: exercise doesn't inherently cause fat loss. You still need to be in a calorie deficit for that. But exercise strongly supports fat loss, by increasing how many calories you burn and improving your resting metabolic rate.

The Foundation: 3 Full-Body Strength Sessions

The core of this plan is three strength training sessions per week, each 60 minutes or less, training your full body each time. That's plenty to get outstanding results if the sessions are structured well and you're progressively adding challenge over time.

High-Intensity Cardio: Short and Efficient

High-intensity interval training, or HIIT, means alternating short bursts of near-maximal effort with periods of lower intensity, like sprinting for 30 seconds followed by walking for 30 seconds, repeated for several rounds.

HIIT is valuable for people short on time for three specific reasons: it's extremely time-efficient, often producing results comparable to a much longer steady-state cardio session in just 10 to 20 minutes; it's excellent for cardiovascular health by pushing your heart rate near its max; and it creates a larger "afterburn" effect, meaning your body burns more calories recovering from the session for up to 24 hours afterward.

Low-Intensity Cardio: Don't Skip This Either

Low-intensity steady-state activity, walking, light jogging, easy biking, plays a different but equally important role. It keeps you moving and burning calories throughout the day, supports cardiovascular health, and actively aids recovery from your strength sessions by increasing blood flow, which helps clear metabolic byproducts and reduce soreness.

If you work out really hard for an hour three times a week and then sit the majority of the day, that's not ideal. We want to move.

The Complete Weekly Framework

1
3x full-body strength training, 60 min or less
The foundation of the plan. Train your whole body each session for consistent stimulus across the week.
2
2x HIIT sessions, 10-20 minutes
Alternate high and low intensity intervals, like sprinting and walking, for an efficient calorie burn and cardiovascular boost.
3
Daily step goal
Low-intensity movement throughout the day supports recovery and adds meaningful calorie burn without extra fatigue.
Key Takeaways
  • Exercise supports fat loss but doesn't replace the need for a calorie deficit.
  • Three full-body strength sessions a week, under 60 minutes each, is enough to build meaningful muscle and strength.
  • HIIT delivers efficient results in 10-20 minutes, plus a greater "afterburn" effect than steady-state cardio.
  • Low-intensity movement like walking supports recovery and cardiovascular health without adding fatigue.
  • This entire plan fits into a realistic weekly schedule without requiring hours of daily gym time.

This plan works because it covers what actually matters, strength stimulus, efficient cardio, and daily movement, without demanding more time than a busy schedule can realistically give.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the minimum amount of exercise needed to lose fat effectively?

Three full-body strength sessions a week at 60 minutes or less, two short high-intensity interval sessions of 10 to 20 minutes, and a daily step goal covers the core bases without requiring hours in the gym.

Does exercise alone cause fat loss?

No. Fat loss requires a calorie deficit, and exercise doesn't automatically create one. Exercise supports fat loss by increasing energy expenditure and metabolic rate, but it works alongside nutrition, not as a replacement for it.

Why is high-intensity interval training so time-efficient?

HIIT alternates high and low intensity periods, burning a comparable number of calories to a much longer steady-state session in as little as 10 to 20 minutes, while also increasing the calories burned during recovery afterward.

Is low-intensity cardio like walking still worth doing?

Yes. Low-intensity steady-state activity like walking supports calorie burn, improves cardiovascular health, and aids recovery from strength training by increasing blood flow without adding significant fatigue.
