Why You're Still Out of Shape (It's Not Your Plan)

You've tried the diets, the workout apps, the hacks you found online. Dr. Joey explains why none of that was ever the real problem, and what's actually been keeping you stuck.

Dr. Joey Munoz, PhD
· August 11, 2026 · 8 min read
Quick Answer

If you've tried multiple plans and still can't stay in shape, the real issue is rarely the plan itself. It's usually one of three things: resisting the identity shift consistency actually requires, being more attached to the goal than the process, or trying to do it all without any support.

Give me the next few minutes, and I want to explain why you haven't been able to get in shape even though you genuinely want to, and it has nothing to do with your actual nutrition or workout plan. If you've cycled through several programs and none of them stuck, the plan was probably never the real problem.

I'm Dr. Joey Munoz, and after helping hundreds of everyday people finally get in shape and stay there, I've noticed the same three things showing up again and again in the people who stay stuck.

You're Resisting Who You Need to Become

Getting in shape isn't really about following a plan for a set period of time. It's about becoming a healthy, fit person, meaning the healthy behaviors happen as a byproduct of who you are, not as a temporary project you're pushing through.

That requires changing your behaviors and, honestly, your identity. That's difficult, it takes real work, and it doesn't happen overnight. A lot of people aren't willing to face that truth, so instead of doing the work, they spend years chasing hacks: a supplement that promises to blunt your appetite, a trick that promises to speed things up. Those hacks don't work, and realizing you spent years chasing them instead of just doing the work is often the hardest part.

You're More In Love With the Goal Than the Process

The idea of achieving a goal is almost always more attractive than the actual day-to-day work required to get there. Everyone wants to be the person who's already in shape. Very few people are excited about the repetitive, unglamorous daily habits that actually produce that result.

Outcome-Oriented

"Did I lose weight today?" Success is measured against the scale, which fluctuates regardless of effort, making progress feel invisible.

Process-Oriented

"Did I do what I said I'd do today?" Success is measured by adherence: nutrition, training, steps, sleep, which you actually control every single day.

It's not about did I wake up and lose weight today. It's about did I do the things I said I was going to do yesterday to get a little closer to my goal.

People see the highlight reel of someone's transformation. They never see the boring, repetitive grind that produced it, which makes it easy to underestimate how much consistency the process actually demands.

You're Trying to Figure It All Out Alone

The third reason people stay stuck for a long time is trying to do everything without support or guidance. This isn't about willpower or intelligence. Going it alone means every single setback, every confusing piece of conflicting information online, every vacation that throws off your routine, has to be solved entirely by you.

That makes the process slower, more confusing, and far easier to abandon. When you have someone helping you navigate the day-to-day obstacles and keeping you accountable, you're substantially more likely to stay consistent through the parts that would otherwise knock you off track.

Key Takeaways
  • Lasting change requires becoming someone whose habits are different by default, not following a temporary plan.
  • Chasing hacks and shortcuts is often a way to avoid facing that the real work takes time and effort.
  • Measure success by whether you did the process, your nutrition, training, steps, and sleep, not by the scale each day.
  • The unglamorous, repetitive daily habits are what actually produce results, not the highlight-reel moments.
  • Trying to solve every setback alone makes the process harder and easier to quit. Support and accountability change that.

None of this means your next plan needs to be more extreme or more complicated. It means the plan was probably never the missing piece. The identity shift, the process focus, and the support around you are what actually decide whether this time is different.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do I keep failing at getting in shape even with a good plan?

Usually it's not the plan. It's one of three things: resisting the identity change required to become someone who trains and eats well consistently, being more attached to the idea of the goal than the daily process, or trying to figure everything out alone without support.

What does it mean to be process-oriented instead of outcome-oriented?

Process-oriented means you measure success by whether you did the specific actions you committed to, like your nutrition, your workout, your steps, and your sleep, rather than by how close the scale is to your goal on any given day.

Why is behavior change so hard even when you want it?

Behavior change requires becoming a different version of yourself, someone whose habits are different by default, not just following a plan temporarily. That identity shift takes consistent repetition over time, which is why it feels harder than simply knowing what to do.

Does having a coach actually make a difference?

Yes, largely because trying to figure everything out alone means absorbing every setback by yourself, which makes the process slower, more confusing, and easier to abandon. Support and accountability tend to significantly increase consistency over time.
