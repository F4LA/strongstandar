Is Weight Loss Impossible for Some People?

A viral clip says weight loss simply isn't possible for some people. Dr. Joey shares his view, and the activity and nutrition habits he uses with clients who start from a hard place.

Dr. Joey Munoz, PhD
·September 14, 2026·6 min read
Quick Answer

Dr. Joey says it isn't true that weight loss is impossible. For people who are very inactive, he starts with movement, a daily step goal and resistance training two to three times a week, before changing the diet. Then he builds flexible nutrition habits like regular meals, protein and fiber at every meal, and whole foods, which often produce weight loss without strict calorie counting.

Dr. Joey recently saw a viral social media clip arguing that pursuing weight loss simply isn't possible for some people. He decided to share his thoughts, with the caveat that his goal isn't to shame anyone or tell people what to do. He does acknowledge that some people deal with hunger that is hard to regulate when they try to lose weight, which he hasn't experienced himself.

At the same time, as a health coach, he has worked with dozens of overweight and obese people who started in a similar position and lost weight sustainably. His experience is the basis for a different answer: it isn't impossible, but where you start matters.

Step One: Start With Movement, Not the Diet

Many people who struggle to lose weight are very inactive. Cutting food intake without exercising is difficult, because your body burns little energy and a lower calorie intake means you barely eat anything. You end up very hungry, which can contribute to binging, weight regain, and unhealthy eating behaviors.

1
Set a daily step goal
Pick a number you can actually achieve, then raise it slowly over time.
2
Start resistance training
Aim for at least two or three sessions per week to build muscle and improve body composition.

These two habits raise your energy expenditure and also help regulate hunger and satiety signals, which makes weight loss feel somewhat easier.

Step Two: Build Flexible Nutrition Habits

Once an exercise routine is in place, Dr. Joey moves to nutrition. He doesn't believe a calorie deficit has to feel extremely restrictive or like significant under-eating. The key is to avoid an all-or-nothing mindset that sorts foods into good and bad and tries to avoid the bad ones at all costs. That approach leads to frustration and failure.

All foods can fit in a long-term fat loss plan. The focus instead is on a handful of habits.

Structure

Eat on a regular meal schedule, plan meals ahead, and start the day with a well-balanced breakfast.

Food choices

Prioritize protein and fiber at each meal, eat mostly whole foods, include fruits and vegetables daily, and limit liquid calories, including alcohol.

With these habits in place, there is still plenty of room for favorites like ice cream or chocolate in moderation.

Why This Often Works Without Counting Calories

If someone goes from being completely sedentary to moving more, lifting weights, and following these habits, they will likely lose weight without counting calories. The behaviors raise energy expenditure, improve fullness, and tend to lower calorie intake without it feeling very restrictive.

Behavior change is hard, and Dr. Joey knows it. But he finds that telling people weight loss is impossible for some people can be harmful, because it may discourage anyone who relates to the message from even trying to improve their health after watching a random video online.

If you have a history of disordered eating or find that dieting triggers intense, hard-to-manage hunger, work with a qualified healthcare professional before starting a deficit.

Key Takeaways
  • Dr. Joey says weight loss isn't impossible, even for people who start inactive and struggle with it.
  • Start with movement: a realistic daily step goal and resistance training two to three times a week.
  • Avoid sorting foods into good and bad, since all foods can fit in a sustainable plan.
  • Focus on habits like regular meals, protein and fiber at each meal, whole foods, and fewer liquid calories.
  • Behavior change that raises activity and fullness can lead to weight loss without strict calorie counting.

The message that weight loss is impossible can leave people feeling stuck before they begin. A better starting point is small, achievable habits, built in the right order, with professional support when you need it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it impossible for some people to lose weight?

Dr. Joey says no. He has helped dozens of overweight and obese people lose weight sustainably, usually by starting with movement and then building flexible nutrition habits.

Why does he start with exercise instead of diet?

Very inactive people burn little energy, so cutting food alone leaves them extremely hungry, which can contribute to binging and regain. More movement raises expenditure and helps regulate hunger and fullness.

What exercise does he recommend first?

A realistic daily step goal that increases slowly, plus resistance training at least two to three times a week to build muscle and improve body composition.

Do you have to count calories to lose weight?

Not always. Moving more, lifting weights, and building habits like regular meals, protein and fiber, and whole foods often lead to eating fewer calories without strict tracking.
