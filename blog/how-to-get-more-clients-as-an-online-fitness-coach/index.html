How to Get More Clients as an Online Fitness Coach

You post consistently, you get clients in the door, and then they quietly disappear. Cody McBroom, who has run an online coaching business for eight and a half years, breaks down the exact sequence that turns good coaching into a business that grows on its own.

Dr. Joey Munoz, PhD
· July 27, 2026 · 7 min read
CM
Cody McBroom
Online fitness coach of 16 years, founder of a coaching business running for over eight years. Now mentors other coaches on building sustainable, results-driven fitness businesses.

You know how to coach. Your clients get results when they stick around. But retention is inconsistent, referrals feel like luck instead of a system, and every time growth stalls you find yourself scrolling for the next marketing tactic: a new funnel, a different offer structure, a paid ad strategy someone swears by.

I sat down with Cody McBroom, who has been coaching online for 16 years and running his own coaching business for over eight, to talk about why this happens and what actually fixes it. His answer surprised me, because it had nothing to do with marketing at all.

Why Chasing Tactics Keeps You in the Same Spot

Cody sees the same pattern constantly in the coaches he mentors. Someone runs a big promotion, signs a wave of clients on a paid in full offer, and it works. Then they try to run the exact same promotion again a few months later, and it falls flat. Worse, the clients they signed the first time don't resign. They can't figure out why.

His answer to them is always the same: before you market, you have to focus on retaining. Marketing without a retention problem solved just refills a leaking bucket faster.

This matters because of something Cody said that reframes the whole problem. Anyone with an Instagram account and a decent physique can call themselves an online coach now. That has flooded the space with people who jump straight to funnels and offers without ever solving the actual thing a coaching business is built on: keeping clients long enough, and happy enough, that they tell other people.

The Sequence That Actually Builds a Coaching Business

Cody teaches his mentees a five step sequence he calls the 5 Rs. The order matters more than any individual step, because skipping ahead is exactly what creates the leaking bucket problem.

1
Results
If your clients aren't getting results, nothing else on this list works. This is the foundation. Cody is direct about it: if you can't get results, your product is the problem, not your marketing.
2
Reviews
Real results should naturally produce reviews, but you still have to ask for them in the right places, at the right moment, from the right clients.
3
Referrals
Cody has never met a coaching business with great results that had to beg for referrals. They come on their own once results and reviews are in place.
4
Reactivations
This isn't mass DMing. It's reaching out to people who already showed interest or already know you, and reopening a real conversation.
5
Request
Only at the end do you teach clients and followers organic calls to action, because by this point you've earned the right to ask.

Notice what isn't on that list: ad spend, funnels, or a new offer structure. Those things can amplify a business that already has the first three Rs working. They cannot fix a business that doesn't.

What Skipping Ahead Actually Costs You

Cody's client base is a good example of what happens when the sequence works in the right order. About 10% of his clients come from Kuwait, not because of any targeted campaign, but because his business partner delivered a great result to one person there, and that person told fifteen others. Cody's own roster grew the same way through word of mouth after he helped one client's friend with a shoulder issue, who turned out to be connected to a network of professional wrestlers.

Our clients' results are kind of like our business card. You never know who your clients know.

Neither of those growth stories started with a marketing tactic. They started with a result good enough that someone felt compelled to talk about it. That's the piece coaches skip when they go straight to trying to fix their funnel.

Putting the 5 Rs to Work This Month

If you're stuck, the fix isn't a new tactic, it's an honest audit of where you actually are in the sequence. Ask yourself these questions in order, and stop at the first one you can't answer with confidence:

  • Are my current clients getting a result they'd tell a friend about?
  • Am I actively asking satisfied clients for reviews, or hoping they volunteer one?
  • When a client refers someone, do I have a simple way to say thank you and keep the relationship warm?
  • Do I have a list of past leads and inactive clients I could reasonably reach out to this week?
  • Am I only asking for new business after I've delivered, not before?

Whichever question stopped you is where your actual work is. Fix that before you touch anything related to marketing.

Key Takeaways
  • Marketing amplifies a business that already retains clients. It can't fix one that doesn't.
  • The 5 Rs go in order: Results, Reviews, Referrals, Reactivations, Request. Skipping ahead is what creates the leaking bucket.
  • A single client with a great result can bring you a dozen more without you spending on ads.
  • Reactivating warm leads is different from mass outreach. It's reopening real conversations with people who already know you.
  • Before you ask for anything, audit honestly whether you've actually earned it yet.

The coaches who grow without burning out on ad spend and constant funnel tweaks are the ones who treat the 5 Rs as a sequence, not a menu. Results come first because everything else is downstream of them. Get that right, and the referrals stop feeling like something you have to chase.
