How Dr. Joey Overcame a Difficult Childhood

If you feel stuck, or like your life is a mess right now, this isn't the story you'd expect from someone who now holds a PhD and runs a business he loves. Dr. Joey shares 3 lessons from rebuilding his life from the ground up.

Dr. Joey Munoz, PhD
· August 15, 2026 · 7 min read
Quick Answer

A difficult past doesn't determine your future. Three things changed the trajectory of Dr. Joey's life: recognizing that his past could shape but not define his future, developing self-awareness to consciously break harmful patterns, and understanding that confidence and success are built through consistent repetition, not something you're simply born with.

If you feel stuck right now, or like your life is a mess, this isn't a story about someone who had it easy. On paper, I was never supposed to have the life I have today.

I grew up in a broken home. My dad struggled with drug and alcohol addiction and was physically abusive toward my mom, something I witnessed repeatedly as a kid. My mom, while not physically abusive, was deeply emotionally abusive in her own way. I did poorly in school, I was overweight, and for a long time I genuinely believed I wasn't going to become anybody.

Today, I have a PhD in nutritional sciences, graduated top of my class, have a healthy marriage and two kids I love, and run a business I'm genuinely proud of. The only reason I'm here is because I chose, deliberately, to do things differently. Here are the three lessons that made that possible.

Your Past Can Shape You, But It Doesn't Have to Define You

My dad told me school was pointless and discouraged me from putting effort into it, so I didn't. I nearly failed out of college in my first two years, twice failing out of my original major before switching to nutrition. I had no structure, no responsibility, and no real direction.

Picture that story next to someone who eventually earns a PhD. Your past absolutely shapes you, but it's not a fixed script for where you end up. What you do with it from this point forward is still yours to decide.

Self-Awareness Is the Foundation for Change

Of everything that's helped me take a difficult past and turn it into something healthy, self-awareness has mattered the most. Without it, you have no real way of knowing which relationships, habits, or patterns in your life are actually working against you, and change becomes something left entirely to chance.

A lot of people who grew up with abusive parents become abusive parents. It doesn't have to be that way. It starts with self-awareness.

Self-awareness is specifically what allowed me to reflect on the exact ways I was parented that caused harm, and to consciously choose not to repeat those same patterns with my own kids. That doesn't happen automatically. It requires actually sitting with your past honestly enough to name what hurt, so you can choose differently.

Confidence and Success Are Built, Not Given

Confidence and success aren't things you're simply born with. They're built through repetition, through doing hard things over and over, failing, and getting back up anyway. Self-awareness alone isn't enough if you're not also willing to be consistent and persistent when things get difficult.

Most people see someone's success and assume it came easily. What they don't see is the long string of failures behind it. The difference between people who eventually succeed and people who don't usually isn't talent, it's simply that one group didn't quit, and got smarter about the direction they were failing in.

Key Takeaways
  • A difficult or abusive past can shape your future, but it doesn't have to determine or define it.
  • Self-awareness is what lets you recognize harmful patterns and consciously choose not to repeat them.
  • Breaking generational patterns requires honest reflection on specifically what caused harm, not just good intentions.
  • Confidence and success are built through consistent repetition and persistence, not something you're simply born with.
  • Most visible success is preceded by a long, often invisible string of failures that the person chose not to quit through.

If any part of this resonates with you, I want you to hear this clearly: it's not too late to rewrite the direction your life is heading. It starts with being honest with yourself about where you actually are, and being willing to keep showing up, even when it's hard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a difficult childhood determine your future?

No. A difficult past can shape your future, but it doesn't have to determine it. Dr. Joey's own path from a broken, abusive home to a PhD and a business he loves is an example of using self-awareness to consciously shape a different direction.

Why is self-awareness described as the foundation for change?

Without self-awareness, you don't know which relationships, habits, or patterns are actually holding you back, so change is left to chance. Self-awareness lets you recognize harmful patterns from your past and deliberately choose not to repeat them.

Are confidence and success something you're born with?

Not primarily. While some people may have natural tendencies, confidence and success are largely built through repetition and consistency, doing hard things over and over, failing, and getting back up rather than giving up.

How did breaking a generational pattern of abuse actually happen?

It required deliberately reflecting on specific ways he was parented that caused harm, and consciously choosing not to repeat those same patterns with his own kids, rather than assuming the cycle would break on its own.
