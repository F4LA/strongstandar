Navigate Holidays Without Losing Progress

You're nervous the holidays are going to undo everything you've worked for. Dr. Allan Bacon shares the mental framework and practical strategies to enjoy yourself without feeling out of control.

Dr. Joey Munoz, PhD
· August 30, 2026 · 7 min read

Dr. Allan Bacon

Quick Answer

Set the expectation that holding your weight steady through a major holiday period is a win, not a failure. Avoid swinging between overly strict "clean eating" and full indulgence, and give yourself permission to enjoy the event with a loose plan in place rather than either extreme.

If you're anxious about the holidays derailing your nutrition and training progress, you're not alone, and this is often the exact point where people starting a fitness journey hit their first real stumbling block. Dr. Allan Bacon shares the mental framework and practical strategies that make this period navigable without sacrificing enjoyment.

Why "Clean Eating" or "Cheat Day" Extremes Cause Problems

A lot of people approach nutrition with only two modes: strict clean eating, or full indulgence once they decide to "cheat." Dr. Bacon points to this all-or-nothing thinking as part of what drives broader struggles with weight, since it skips over the moderate middle ground where sustainable habits actually live.

Finding a happy medium is really what we should be shooting for. That happy medium allows us to indulge a bit, but we set a guideline that gives us permission without experiencing loss of control.

Set the Right Expectation First

Before worrying about specific food choices, Dr. Bacon recommends adjusting your expectations. If your overall goal is fat loss, holding your weight steady through a major holiday week is a genuine win, not a failure.

What Feels Like Failure

Seeing the scale up a pound or two after a holiday and assuming it represents real fat gain.

What's Actually Happening

New or varied foods often mean more sodium and carbs than usual, which shows up as water and glycogen, not fat.

Don't Wait for a "Clean" Time to Start

Many people delay starting a health journey until after the holidays, assuming things will be easier once the season ends. Dr. Bacon suggests the opposite: navigating social eating situations is a skill you'll eventually need regardless of timing, so working through a coach or plan during these periods can actually be valuable practice rather than something to avoid.

Zoom Out: One Week Rarely Matters as Much as It Feels Like It Does

If your goal involves losing considerable weight, that's likely a process spanning many months, sometimes over a year. Within that timeline, the vast majority of days are ordinary, non-holiday days. Dr. Bacon encourages focusing intense effort and consistency on those mundane days rather than fixating on a single holiday week derailing everything.

Key Takeaways
  • Holding your weight steady through a holiday period is a genuine win when your goal is fat loss.
  • Swinging between strict clean eating and full indulgence skips the sustainable middle ground.
  • A pound or two of weight gain after a holiday is often water and glycogen, not actual fat.
  • Navigating social eating is a skill worth practicing rather than avoiding until a "clean" period.
  • Most days of the year are ordinary. Consistency on those days matters more than perfection during holidays.

The holidays don't have to be an obstacle course you either survive or fail. With the right expectations and a loose plan that allows real enjoyment, they can be exactly what they're meant to be, without derailing months of progress.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's a realistic goal for the holidays if you're trying to lose weight?

Holding your weight steady through a major holiday period is a genuine win, not a failure. Even if the scale shows a pound or two up, it's often water, sodium, and glycogen rather than actual fat gain, especially if you're eating foods you don't normally eat.

Why does the "clean eating vs. cheat day" mindset backfire during holidays?

Swinging between extremely strict eating and full indulgence removes the middle ground where sustainable habits actually live. A more moderate approach that allows planned indulgence tends to protect both progress and enjoyment better than an all-or-nothing mindset.

Should you avoid social events entirely while trying to lose weight?

No. Learning to navigate social eating situations is a skill you'll eventually need regardless of when you start. Waiting for a "clean" period with no events to begin your health journey often means never starting at all.

How much does one holiday week actually affect a long-term fitness goal?

Very little in the context of a goal that takes months or years. Most days of the year are ordinary, non-holiday days, and it's more productive to focus consistent effort on those mundane days than to fixate on perfection during a single week.
