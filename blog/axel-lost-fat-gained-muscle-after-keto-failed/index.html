How Axel Lost Fat and Gained Muscle After Keto Failed

Axel watched friends get lean on keto and gave it a try himself. Instead of losing weight, he plateaued, then started gaining it back. Here's what finally worked, four months later.

Quick Answer

Axel's keto attempt worked for about three weeks, then plateaued and reversed into weight gain by week six. What actually worked was four months of structured coaching that fit his job, his family life, and his recovery from a serious health issue, resulting in 22 pounds of fat lost and 6 pounds of muscle gained.

Axel Rubio, an executive based in Luxembourg, had considered himself a health-conscious person for years. But a demanding office job, a busy family life, and eventually a serious long-term health issue pushed his habits in the wrong direction, and he found himself looking for real answers, not just more information.

Like a lot of people, his first real attempt at change came from watching friends succeed with something and deciding to copy it. That decision is where his story really starts.

Why Keto Worked, Until It Didn't

Two Christmases ago, friends visiting Axel raved about the keto diet, describing rapid weight loss while skipping breakfast and staying in ketosis. Trusting people he knew meant well, Axel researched it, bought ketone test strips, and gave it a try himself.

The first three weeks felt like validation: he was losing weight, and it felt like he'd finally found the answer. By week four, progress stalled. By week six, the trend reversed and he started gaining weight back. By week seven, he felt sluggish and heavier than when he started.

Four weeks into it, I was like, well, I'm not losing any more weight. Six weeks into it, it was going the opposite. I was still gaining weight.

The approach that worked dramatically for his friends simply didn't hold up for his own body and lifestyle, a reminder that a diet's popularity has very little to do with whether it's the right fit for the person trying it.

A Nutritionist Wasn't Automatically the Answer Either

Before finding structured coaching, Axel had already worked with a nutritionist in Luxembourg for several months. Despite that professional relationship, the approach wasn't producing the results he needed, which left him skeptical about trying coaching again at all.

Being cautious about who to trust with something this personal, he spent months researching before choosing to work with Fit for Life Academy, ultimately deciding the structure and approach mattered more than credentials alone.

Four Months, Measurable Results

Working through structured coaching over four months, layered on top of recovering from a serious health issue, Axel's results were substantial both physically and metabolically.

Beyond the physical numbers, Axel reported improvements in blood sugar levels, insulin resistance, and triglycerides, markers directly tied to the health issue that had originally pushed him to seek real change.

The Part He Didn't Expect: Inspiring the People Around Him

One of the results Axel didn't anticipate was the effect his consistency had on his own family. His wife, who had been resistant to resistance training for years and always framed it as "his thing," started joining his training sessions after watching his progress over several weeks.

His sister also decided to make her own health changes after seeing the shift in him. Consistency, sustained long enough to actually show, tends to influence the people closest to you more than any conversation about it ever could.

Key Takeaways
  • Keto produced early results for Axel, but plateaued by week four and reversed into weight gain by week six.
  • A diet working for someone else doesn't guarantee it fits your own body, lifestyle, or preferences.
  • Working with a nutritionist beforehand wasn't automatically effective. The right structure mattered more than the credential alone.
  • Over four months, structured coaching produced 22 lbs of fat loss and 6 lbs of muscle gain simultaneously.
  • Sustained personal consistency ended up inspiring people close to him, including his wife and sister, to make their own changes.

Axel's story is a reminder that a diet's popularity isn't the same as its fit. What worked for his friends actively worked against him, and it took a structured, personalized approach, not another trending diet, to actually move the numbers that mattered.
