Lose Weight After 40 Without Losing Muscle

If the eat-less-move-more approach that used to work has stopped working, the problem isn't willpower. It's the plan. Here's the exact protocol I give my clients over 40.

Dr. Joey Munoz, PhD
· July 21, 2026 · 7 min read

You're over 40, and the strategy that used to work, eat a little less, move a little more, has stopped delivering. You cut calories. You added cardio. Maybe you lost a few pounds and then stalled, or lost them and watched them creep back within a few months. It's easy to conclude your body is broken or that you're simply too old now. I hear this constantly, and I want to be direct with you: it's not true.

What's actually happening is that your body has changed, which means the approach has to change too. I want to walk you through exactly why the old strategy stopped working and give you the specific numbers I use with my own clients to fix it.

Why Eat Less, Move More Stops Working

Starting in your 30s, hormonal shifts begin working against you. In men, testosterone declines roughly 1 to 2% per year. In women, estrogen drops sharply through peri- and post-menopause, which affects hunger regulation and how your body stores fat. Both shifts accelerate the loss of muscle mass, typically 3 to 8% per decade if you're not actively working against it, and restrictive dieting without the right nutritional strategy makes that loss worse.

Here's why that matters so much: muscle is metabolically active tissue. Think of it as the engine size of your car. A bigger engine burns through more fuel. As that engine shrinks with age and muscle loss, you burn less fuel at rest, so the same eating pattern that worked at 30 now leaves you overfed at 45.

Your body isn't broken. It's changing. The muscle you're losing every decade is the actual reason your metabolism feels slower, not your age.

The Goal Isn't the Scale, It's Muscle

I want to be direct about this, because it changes everything downstream: weight loss and fat loss are not the same thing. When you chase the number on the scale through hard restriction, heavy cardio, and cutting calories aggressively, you will lose weight, but a meaningful chunk of it comes from muscle, not fat.

Lose muscle, and your metabolic rate drops further. Drop your metabolic rate, and you have to eat even less to keep losing, which means you're hungry constantly and stuck in a cycle of restriction that feels like it takes enormous willpower to sustain. This is why so many people describe weight loss after 40 as feeling like a fight against their own body.

Wrong Target
Chase the Scale
Heavy restriction and cardio drop the number fast, but a large share of that loss is muscle. Metabolism slows further, hunger increases, and the weight returns.
Right Target
Protect the Muscle
A slower, muscle-preserving approach keeps your metabolic rate up, so you can eat more food while still losing fat, and the results actually hold.

When you shift the target to muscle instead of weight, you stop needing to starve yourself to see progress. You fuel your body properly to recover from training, your metabolic rate improves as you lose fat, and the whole process feels sustainable instead of like a constant battle.

Build the Foundation Before You Touch a Number

Before you get anywhere near calories or macros, I have every client build the same foundational habits. This is the part most people skip, and it's exactly why diets that jump straight to restriction fall apart.

  • Eat whole foods 80 to 85% of the time, no food groups eliminated
  • Hit 25 to 30 grams of fiber daily for hunger and satiety regulation
  • Get at least 5 servings of fruits and vegetables per day for micronutrients
  • Build a well-balanced breakfast and front-load food earlier in the day

Micronutrients matter more than people give them credit for. Something like magnesium, which the majority of people are deficient in, directly affects hormone function, exercise recovery, and how your body builds muscle. Skip the foundation, and none of the numbers below will work the way they should.

The Exact Numbers: Protein and Calorie Targets

Once the foundation is in place, my clients tailor two variables to their goal: protein intake and calorie deficit. Protein is non-negotiable, it's the building block your body uses to preserve and build muscle while you're losing fat. The target is 0.8 grams per pound of your ideal body weight, not your current weight.

Protein Target Example
Current weight250 lbs
Ideal body weight goal200 lbs
Multiplier× 0.8 g/lb
Daily protein target160 g/day

For the calorie side, I'm always a bigger fan of a slower deficit because it's more sustainable and protects muscle better. Target a deficit that produces about 0.5% of your body weight in fat loss per week, which works out to roughly 15 to 20% below maintenance for most people.

Calorie Deficit Example
Maintenance calories2,000 cal
Target deficit15–20%
Daily calorie target1,600–1,700 cal
Expected weekly fat loss~1 lb/week

That pace is enough to see consistent, visible progress without dropping so low that you're sacrificing muscle, missing your nutrient needs, or white-knuckling your way through the week.

Training Has to Build Muscle, Not Just Burn Calories

Exercise in this equation isn't primarily about burning calories, it's about building muscle, and that means resistance training has to be at the center of your plan. Cardio and yoga are fine additions, but lifting weights incorrectly, think circuit classes with light dumbbells and 30-second rounds, doesn't reliably stimulate muscle growth. You need to train with real intent behind the movement, the same way you'd need to train specifically to become a marathon runner rather than just going for the occasional jog.

Key Takeaways
  • Your body isn't broken after 40, it's changing, and the strategy has to change with it.
  • Weight loss and fat loss aren't the same. Chasing the scale sacrifices muscle and slows your metabolism further.
  • Build the foundation first: whole foods, fiber, produce, and a solid breakfast, before touching a single number.
  • Protein target: 0.8 g per pound of your ideal body weight, daily and consistent.
  • Calorie target: a modest 15 to 20% deficit, aiming for about 0.5% of body weight lost per week.

None of this requires perfection. Stay consistent with these habits and numbers 80 to 90% of the time, allow yourself flexibility the rest of the time, and build your plan around foods you actually enjoy eating. That's what makes it sustainable enough to still be working a year from now, which is the whole point.
