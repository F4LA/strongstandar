The Story

Meet Jennifer

Jennifer is 60. She spent 25 years as a data analyst before recently opening her own business, taking her first steps as an entrepreneur. In her own words, she was a "hard gainer" through her twenties, and didn't consistently weigh 100 pounds until after giving birth to her third of three children.

Weight was never really an issue for her until her fifties. Then it started creeping up slowly, especially after she broke her leg. By the time she reached out to us, she was less bothered by the number on the scale than by how uncomfortable she felt in her own body, on top of low energy that was affecting her day to day.

She'd been following Dr. Joey's content for over a year and was drawn to its science-based, no-nonsense approach. In February 2026, she decided it was time to commit.

The Challenges

What She Was Up Against

In Jennifer's own words, getting to the gym was never her problem. Nutrition was.

❌"Convenience is a big thing for me, and I hate cooking," she says. She disliked cooking and meal prep enough to pay for expensive delivery services like Cook Unity just to avoid the stove.
❌On the workout side, she describes herself as "bouncing around, trying different things, getting bored," never giving anything long enough to see real outcomes.
❌Past approaches were never built around her real schedule or her real dislike of cooking, so nothing stuck.
❌Even mid-program, ordinary weight fluctuations around travel and family visits tested her confidence in the process.

It wasn't for lack of effort. The direction was not there yet.

The Turning Point

When It Finally Clicked

That's when Jennifer started working with us, in February 2026. We didn't hand her a generic meal plan. We paired her with Miguel, a coach who happens to be a chef, precisely because her biggest obstacle was the kitchen itself.

Her foundational phase was condensed to four weeks based on her experience, built around two habits first: eating on a regular schedule and hitting protein at every meal.

The plan worked because it removed friction instead of adding rules. Miguel built her a "barebones" meal-prep guide around an air fryer, a rotisserie chicken, and pre-chopped vegetables, things she already owned and could actually use. Every week she checked in and got personal video feedback back the next day. The plan kept adjusting through a full fat-loss phase, into maintenance, back into fat loss again, with training tweaks along the way.

The plan also flexed around her actual life instead of competing with it: a family trip to New York, a great-granddaughter's visit full of paddleboarding, skating, and bike riding, and a new gym membership for heavier equipment, all absorbed without derailing her progress. When she opened her own Ace Handyman Services franchise partway through the program, she chose to hold off on a longer contract with us so she could focus on the new business first, and the coaching adjusted around that decision rather than around a sales conversation.

Looking Back

Jennifer's Reflection

What changed for Jennifer wasn't just the scale. It was her relationship with the one thing she was sure would never change: cooking. Where she once paid to avoid her own kitchen, she now has a system that works for her, even while running a brand-new business.

She's honest that the results aren't dramatic. What she'll tell you is that they're real, and that they came from finally finding something that fit her actual life.

In Jennifer's Words

"The program worked. I've seen changes. They're not eye-popping by any means, but I've definitely seen the changes. If someone was feeling like me: nothing ever changes if nothing ever changes. You've got to find something that resonates and can fit into your life, something sustainable. It's well worth it, and I'm happy that I did it."

Key Takeaways

What Jennifer's Story Teaches Us

✅A plan that fights your real dislikes, like cooking, will always lose. A plan built around them can actually stick.
✅Menopause-driven weight changes are not a life sentence. They respond to the same fundamentals, applied consistently.
✅Big life changes, like starting a business, do not have to derail progress when the plan is built to flex around them.
✅Progress can include trying something completely new, like pickleball, and being genuinely bad at it for a while.
