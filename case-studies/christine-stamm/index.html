The Story

Meet Christine

Christine is 55, married, and just retired from a career in IT. She's run half marathons and a number of full marathons, and she has no interest in slowing down. Her goals were clear: lean up, get more agile for her running, and build the muscle that protects her from injury as she ages.

She was never a dieter. She ate a lot of good food and wanted to lower her risk for disease. She even strength trained, because in the running world everyone talks about being resilient to injury. But she wasn't getting results, and she wasn't sure she was doing it right. "How do I know how heavy to go? How do I know what to do?"

Meanwhile the weight kept creeping up, until she'd reached her highest point in years. Her clothes were tight, and running felt less comfortable. She started to wonder if this was just what getting older looked like: "Maybe women as they get older, you just need to accept some weight gain... Maybe I need different size clothes."

The Turning Point

Putting Real Weight On The Bar

She'd been watching Dr. Joey's content for a while because it felt evidence-based, practical, and nuanced, and she'd thought about reaching out more than once. There was always something going on. Then she retired, and the excuses ran out. "Well, I'm retired. I can try this... If it isn't for me, I don't have to keep going." She joined our challenge, bought her own Olympic bar, and started putting real weight on it.

From there she started one-on-one coaching with Coach Jackie. Every week she sent a check-in and videos of her lifts, and every week Jackie sent back technique feedback and adjustments. Her nutrition was built around her running instead of against it: lighter days during the week, and more carbs to fuel her long weekend runs. In her words, "there's no expectation to do everything perfect... It's about making progress, not perfection."

Then life piled on. An eye that needed time to heal, and later, eye surgery. A hamstring injury that cut back her running right before a race, and a second opinion from a runner's physical therapist. A tetanus shot painful enough to feel in her workouts. A medication change for a condition she's managed for 25 years, and sleep that wouldn't cooperate. Jackie adjusted her workouts around each one instead of shutting everything down, and even raised her calories to put performance first for her race. She kept losing anyway.

Looking Back

Christine's Reflection

Learning to lift heavy meant feeling awkward at first, and she gave herself grace for it. "I'm not gonna be perfect the first time or the second time, but I'm gonna learn. I'm gonna get better." Her coach saw the same thing week after week: when the worst-case scenarios started piling up while she waited on that second opinion, she caught it, stayed calm, and went back to what she could control. Today she just feels more confident, and she knows she has the tools to keep getting stronger and leaner over time.
